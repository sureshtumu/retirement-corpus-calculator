How this calculator works

The question

This tool answers one thing: will the money you save last? It works out your Magic Number — the corpus you need at retirement so your lifestyle is funded all the way to your plan-until age — and shows whether you're on track or facing a gap, live, as you move any control.

Everything runs in your own browser. Nothing you type ever leaves your device.

One salary, many claims

Each rupee you earn is pulled several ways at once — income tax, living expenses, loan EMIs, your children's goals, and retirement savings. Savings is simply whatever is left after the rest. So a bigger loan means less saved in those years, and correctly builds a smaller corpus — borrowing has an honest cost here.

Income tax, computed live

The tax row isn't editable — it applies the current New Regime slabs, the standard deduction, and the Section 87A rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh a year effectively tax-free. At ₹1 lakh a month your tax is near zero; it rises by slab as you earn more. Move the income slider and watch it recalculate.

Loans in rupees — the affordability engine

Set each loan in actual rupees. The tool sizes the EMI (compound / reducing-balance, as banks do) and keeps your total EMIs under a tax-aware debt ceiling: up to 40% of salary while you earn ₹2 lakh a month or less, tightening to 30% above that. Loan defaults scale with your income, and every slider starts at zero — so if you have no education loan, or bought your home outright, just leave it at zero.

Over the ceiling? The tool shows exactly what would fit, and the Auto-fit button snaps your loans to eligible amounts in one click — or you drag them yourself. Nothing changes without your click.

Salary growth vs lifestyle growth — the secret

Two separate sliders. Salary growth is how fast you earn. Lifestyle growth is how fast your spending rises — usually slower. The gap between them is what builds your wealth: a raise you don't fully spend becomes retirement savings.

Try it: raise salary growth to 10% while lifestyle stays at 5%. Your funded % climbs — because earning more, without spending it all, genuinely gets you ahead.

Your retirement need is anchored to your slower-growing lifestyle, not your full last-drawn salary — because a disciplined earner never consumed all of it.

Children as goals, not loans

Education and marriage have deadlines you know decades ahead. Saved for from the start, they cost a tiny fraction of income each month — far less than borrowing late.

After retirement — buckets and tax

The corpus splits into three buckets that always total 100%: an Annuity (principal locked, pays income for life, returned in full to your nominee on death), FD/Liquid, and Equity/MF. Drag any share and FD absorbs the change; annuity stays put. Each year is funded from FD, refilled from equity to keep a cash cushion.

Tax in retirement is charged only on income — annuity payout, FD interest, and realised equity gains — never on the principal you draw back, since that's your own money returning.

The honesty test — Monte Carlo

Flat averages flatter. The tool runs your whole life 1,000 times with real market ups and downs and reports how many survive to your plan-until age. A plan that looks well-funded on paper can be a coin-flip in practice — that gap is the lesson.

A few honest limits

This is an educational what-if illustration, not investment advice — please consult a financial advisor for decisions. The tool works in yearly steps and simplifies some real-world tax detail. Nothing here is a return guarantee.

Retirement Corpus — The Magic Number

Every rupee of income at your starting age is split into expense and accumulation lots. Lots inflate with a flat index; accumulation compounds until retirement; the corpus then funds retirement expenses. Change any number — everything recalculates instantly.

How it computes. Income lots inflate each year by the index. Accumulation lots are monthly amounts (₹5/month → ₹60/year, as in your note), added yearly and compounded at the accumulation return until retirement. Required corpus is the amount at retirement that exactly funds each year's inflated retirement expense (expense ratio applied) with the remainder earning the post-retirement return, ending at zero at the plan-until age. Salary grows at its own rate; savings ride on the salary and the retirement lifestyle is anchored to the last-drawn salary, then inflates at price inflation (healthcare at its own rate). At retirement the corpus deploys into buckets: the annuity share is locked for life (income only, principal to nominee), each retirement year is funded from the FD/liquid bucket, which is then topped back up to the cash-cover level by selling equity — so there is always ready cash and equity keeps working; the magic number is found by search — the smallest corpus that survives the plan under the same bucket policy, after pension or rent income. When a loan or goal with a year-slider ends before retirement, its freed share automatically flows into a matching "Freed when … ends" savings tranche from that very year — shorten an EMI and watch the corpus respond. Edit or Remove the freed tranche to redirect less than 100%. Under every category name the grey line shows the exact span the formula uses — by default Education Loan runs 15 yrs (25→40), House Loan EMI 30 yrs (25→55), Child Education & Marriage 35 yrs (25→60) — and it updates the moment you change From / Till / retirement ages.
